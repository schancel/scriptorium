Type every letter?

You have been typing fast enough that the greyed-out letters are probably getting in your way rather than out of it.

Gilding turns them back on. Every character in the passage is required, nothing skips ahead on its own, and the letters your stage has not taught yet turn gold as you type them. Fill a whole part and you earn gold leaf.

It will not move your stage: the mastery gate still measures only the keys your stage teaches, in either mode. If you want to skip ahead, set your stage in the menu — that is the direct way, and it is one control instead of a side effect.

      Your keyboard

      The overlay must match the board under your hands, and the report card has a row for the thumb you actually use.

      Your stage

      The stage decides which letters are lit. Setting it yourself is the honest way to skip ahead if you already type — nothing else moves it except earning it.

      Gilding is for people who already type. With it on, every character is required and nothing skips ahead on its own; the letters your stage has not taught turn gold as you type them, and filling a whole part earns gold leaf. It does not move your stage — the mastery gate measures only the keys your stage teaches, in either mode.

      The ink cloud

      Stop typing altogether and a cloud of ink drifts in and smudges finished work. Taking a long time over a single key never brings it — only stopping does. Turn it off if it nags rather than nudges; nothing else changes, and the game is fully playable without it.

      The route

      The route is a small graph of passages joined by phrases they share, not a run through the Bible front to back. Read without typing is the same page on the same rail with nothing asked of you — it speeds up gently the longer you stay with it, and Esc brings you back.
