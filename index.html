What happens next

Type every letter?

You have been typing fast enough that the dim letters are probably getting in your way rather than out of it.

Gilding turns them back on. Every character in the passage is required, nothing skips ahead on its own, and the letters your stage has not taught yet turn gold as you type them. Gild every verse in a stretch and you earn gold leaf, which multiplies your score for the rest of the chapter.

It will not move your stage. A stage still opens on the keys it teaches, and the letters you gild are not those keys — in either mode. If you want to skip ahead, set your stage in the menu: that is the direct way, and it is one control instead of a side effect.

Either answer is fine and this is the only time you will be asked. The menu has the switch from now on, under Typing the dim letters — and beside it, under When you hit the wrong key, is the other setting for people who already type: let a wrong letter stand and take it back with backspace.               Which text

              Both are the whole Bible and both are free of copyright. What differs is the typing. In the King James roughly one word in thirty carries an archaic ending or pronoun — doeth, knowest, thee, thou, thy, ye — where in the World English Bible it is one in seven hundred. Its sentences also run about half as long again, strung together with colons and semicolons instead of being broken up by full stops, so there is further to type before you get to stop.

              It does not touch your stage. The same letters are lit in either text and the same accuracy is asked of them — you have changed the prose, not the standard. It is the same curriculum in harder words: worth doing once you have been through this one, and available now if you would rather.

              Your keyboard

              The keyboard drawn on screen must match the board under your hands, and the report card has a row for the thumb you actually use.

              Your stage

              The stage decides which letters are lit. Setting it yourself is the honest way to skip ahead if you already type — nothing else moves it except earning it.

              Typing the dim letters

              Some letters on the page are dim. They need keys your stage has not taught yet, so the game types them for you and the cursor skips past them. If you already type, you can ask for all of them instead.

              Gilding is for people who already type. With it on, every character is required and nothing skips ahead on its own; the letters your stage has not taught turn gold as you type them, and gilding every verse in a stretch earns gold leaf, which multiplies your score for the rest of the chapter. It does not move your stage — a stage opens on the keys it teaches, in either mode.

              When you hit the wrong key

              By default a wrong key does not move you along: the cursor waits where it is until you type the letter the page is asking for. If you are still learning where the keys are, leave it there — it stops you typing a whole wrong word without noticing.

              The other setting is what every other keyboard does. The letter you actually typed stands in the cell, marked wrong, and the cursor carries on; backspace takes it back and steps you onto it again. Your accuracy counts every key you press either way, so nothing is hidden by it.

              The ink cloud

              Stop typing altogether and a cloud of ink drifts in and smudges finished work. Taking a long time over a single key never brings it — only stopping does. Turn it off if it nags rather than nudges; nothing else changes, and the game is fully playable without it.

              Movement

              The words slide past a point that never moves, and the scenery behind them slides at three different speeds. After a long sitting that can leave things drifting for a few minutes afterwards — still text seeming to wave, a stopped screen seeming to slide. It is harmless and it passes, but it is a reason to stop, and nobody should have to choose between practising and being comfortable.

              With movement reduced the page steps forward instead of sliding, the scenery behind it holds still, and the flourishes over a passage run gently rather than stopping. Where you read does not change: the letter you are typing stays in the column it has always been in. Nothing is removed — every passage still looks like the place it is set in, and every chapter still plays what it plays.

              Your operating system has a setting for this, and following it is the default. Set it here instead if you want it on one machine and not another.

              Sound

              The control at the bottom of the screen reports the audio device rather than the setting, so on there means the browser is actually playing. Browsers refuse to start audio until you have typed on or clicked the page, and suspend it again whenever the tab goes to the background; typing brings it back. If the game is silent while the control reads on, the line above is the browser's own account of the device, and it is the thing to send us.

              Your hands

              The per-finger table you get after every few verses, over everything you have typed rather than the last handful — plus the worst keys, how far this stage is from opening, and the shape of the last few weeks. It is here because a history you can only see by finishing something is one you cannot look at when you want to.
